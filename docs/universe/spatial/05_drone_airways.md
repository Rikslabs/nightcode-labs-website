# NightCode Campus — Drone Airways

## Principles

Airways occupy a separate vertical layer above public routes and below the most sensitive observation zones. Routes are directional, bounded, and attached to visible operational needs. Drones yield to people at crossings and never become ambient swarms.

## Maintenance airway

Runs from the Energy Core service yard to every primary building through the shortest protected loops. It carries inspection, repair, and supply units. It connects Energy Core, Headquarters, Research, Studio, Games, and JARVIS.

## Research airway

Runs from Research Plateau to JARVIS and the northern reserve. It carries samples, instruments, and observation units. It stays above the research edge and avoids the public civic axis.

## Construction airway

Runs from offshore and western logistics zones to Studio Isle, Research, and future expansion edges. It is the largest route by clearance and is active only during defined building periods.

## Visitor airway

Runs from Visitor Harbor to an arrival platform near Visitor Center, with an optional controlled observation loop. It is slow, quiet, and scenic, and never crosses the main skyline sightline at low altitude.

## Emergency airway

Provides direct high-clearance access between the harbor, Energy Core, Headquarters, and upper districts. It is normally quiet and marked only by dormant guide beacons.

## JARVIS airway

Vertical access around the tower’s service perimeter connects the crown, observation deck, Core, and authorized research routes. It maintains a clear exclusion zone around the main tower silhouette.

## Operational rules

- Airways have defined origins, destinations, altitude bands, and holding points.
- Traffic density decreases near public observation corridors.
- Drones pause at ports and landing courts rather than hovering aimlessly.
- Route lighting indicates permission and direction, not decoration.
- Emergency and construction routes remain visibly distinct from visitor movement.
