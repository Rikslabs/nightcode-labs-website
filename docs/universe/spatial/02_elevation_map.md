# NightCode Campus — Elevation Map

## Geographic datum

Elevations are conceptual above the campus water datum. They describe the intended landform and camera geography, not surveyed construction levels. The vertical sequence gives the visitor a legible climb from public arrival to the active and infrastructural heart of the campus.

## Elevation assignments

| Place | Elevation | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Visitor Harbor | 420 m | Lowest public datum; establishes open-water arrival and the full rise of the campus. |
| Visitor Center | 500 m | First safe terrace above the harbor; allows an immediate overview without intimidation. |
| Headquarters Plaza | 700 m | Civic midpoint and principal redistribution point. |
| Headquarters | 740 m | Slightly above the plaza so the civic building reads as an anchored destination. |
| Energy Core | 760 m | Central operational basin, visible from the plaza but not placed at the skyline crest. |
| Games Basin | 780 m | East-side active terrace with enough separation from focused work. |
| Studio Isle | 820 m | Elevated western production landform, connected by a long, observable crossing. |
| Research Plateau | 860 m | Highest broad working terrace; supports quiet, horizon-facing study. |
| JARVIS Tower base | 880 m | Northeast high ground gives the tower a credible foundation. |
| JARVIS crown | 950 m | Highest permanent landmark; visible from every principal approach. |

## Why the geography matters

The 530 m rise from harbor to tower makes arrival cinematic without requiring artificial scale. The Energy Core remains high enough to be seen and approached, while its surrounding basin lets water and service routes read as systems. Research and Studio rise above Headquarters to imply focused work, but Headquarters remains the emotional center because every major route returns to its plaza.

## Movement implications

Routes do not climb continuously. They alternate between ramps, terraces, bridges, and level courts so that elevation is felt through changing horizons rather than fatigue. Long views should reveal at least one intermediate terrace before the next destination.
