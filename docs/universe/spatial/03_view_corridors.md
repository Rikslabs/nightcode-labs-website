# NightCode Campus — View Corridors

View corridors are protected relationships between locations and landmarks. A corridor may narrow, frame, or temporarily conceal a view, but it must not lose the larger geography without a reason.

| Origin | First visible landmark | What becomes visible next | Intended emotion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arrival Platform | JARVIS crown above the northern ridge | Headquarters roofline and the central waterfall | Orientation, then ambition |

| JARVIS observation deck | Panoramic skyline | Harbor, campus bowl, and future islands | Comprehension and return |

## Skyline rule

The recognizable skyline consists of three reads: low harbor arrival, broad Headquarters mass, and the singular JARVIS vertical. Energy Core light confirms the center after dark. No later structure may obscure all three from the principal arrival corridor.

## Corridor discipline

Primary views use open air, water, or low planting as foreground. Secondary views may be filtered through structure, vegetation, or weather. A landmark is never revealed only through a sign; its geometry must do the orienting work.
