# NightCode Campus — Expansion Strategy

## Principle

Expansion is reserved in the plan before it is designed in detail. Empty territory is evidence of capacity, not unfinished decoration. No reserved zone may be filled speculatively.

## Reserved zones

| Reserve | Conceptual location | Future capability | Current condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| NightCode OS | Northern shelf beyond Research | Operating systems, orchestration, and observability | Unoccupied plateau; preserve research skyline |
| Future AI Campus | North-northeast horizon behind JARVIS | Long-horizon intelligence research and training | Distant landform only; no buildings |
| Robotics | Eastern edge beyond Games Basin | Physical systems, fabrication, and field testing | Open test landscape; retain emergency airway |
| Cloud Infrastructure | Subsurface and offshore around Energy Core | Distributed compute, storage, and network services | Service reserve; visible only through future infrastructure |
| Education | Western horizon beyond Studio Isle | Workshops, residencies, and public learning | Low wooded reserve; preserve Studio causeway view |
| Unknown Products | Outer islands and unassigned horizon | Capabilities not yet named | Empty islands with transfer points only |

## Growth sequence

1. Prove the need within an existing district.
2. Identify the appropriate reserve by capability and relationship.
3. Extend energy, water, service, and emergency infrastructure first.
4. Establish one new landmark and one public threshold.
5. Preserve the Tier 1 skyline and all primary view corridors.
6. Add the district’s material and atmospheric variation only after its purpose is fixed.

## Decade-scale safeguards

The original harbor-to-tower journey remains intact. Expansion occurs at the perimeter, vertically within established tower envelopes, or below grade around the Core. New buildings must connect to a named bridge or airway and must contribute a new function, not merely a new silhouette.

## Unknown future

The unknown reserve is intentionally not themed. It should communicate readiness through landform, docks, and infrastructure capacity while withholding a premature story. This protects the campus from being redesigned around assumptions.
