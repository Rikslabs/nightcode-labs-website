# NightCode Universe — Environment

## Sky

The sky is a deep atmospheric field with enough tonal variation to establish depth. It is a spatial ceiling, not a backdrop image. Horizon conditions must support the campus silhouette.

## Clouds

Clouds are sparse, volumetric, and slow. They provide scale and occlusion, occasionally framing a landmark. They must not obscure primary orientation for extended periods.

## Weather

Weather is a low-frequency environmental state. Mist, rain, wind, and clear conditions modify reflection, sound implication, visibility, and light. Weather never becomes a distracting effect layer or a random mood change.

## Waterfalls

Waterfalls are the most visible expression of the campus water cycle. They mark elevation changes and carry water from the upper landscape toward the Energy Core and surrounding basin. Their persistence is structural; their variation is atmospheric.

## Nature

Nature is selected for silhouette, habitat, seasonality, and relationship to infrastructure. Vegetation gathers along water, shade, and transition zones. It frames routes, softens hard edges, and occasionally reclaims unused areas. It does not conceal every engineered surface.

## Lighting

Lighting follows a physically readable source hierarchy:

```text
sun / sky → reflected environment → building apertures → local operational light
```

Operational light is localized and economical. It indicates activity, safety, entry, energy flow, or information.

## Time of day

The canonical state is late blue hour into night: enough ambient light for geography, enough darkness for energy and interior life to read. Dawn and daylight are valid future states, but transitions must preserve landmarks and material identity.

## Atmosphere

Atmosphere uses distance haze, humidity, reflected light, and sound implication to establish depth. The air should feel cool, clean, and electrically active without becoming sterile.
