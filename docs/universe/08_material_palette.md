# NightCode Universe — Material Palette

## Glass

Glass provides observation, depth, and controlled permeability. It is never a universal skin. Use varied transparency and thickness so interior activity and structure remain legible.

## Titanium

Titanium marks precision, protection, and high-value infrastructure. It appears at frames, joints, signal elements, and durable exposed systems. Its presence is exact, not ornamental.

## Concrete

Concrete establishes mass, permanence, and civic scale. It may be smooth or visibly cast, but it must retain weight and shadow.

## Stone

Stone connects the campus to geology and time. It belongs at foundations, retaining surfaces, public thresholds, and water paths.

## Water

Water is both material and system. It reflects, cools, separates, joins, and demonstrates gravity. Its paths should suggest a larger cycle.

## Vegetation

Vegetation supplies life, season, scale, and recovery. It is varied by district and condition, never a repeated filler layer.

## Energy

Energy is concentrated, directional, and scarce in appearance. It marks active systems and transitions. It does not coat every edge.

## Lighting

Lighting reveals form before it colors form. Cool operational light belongs to systems; warmer light belongs to hospitality, work, and public gathering.

## Reflection

Reflection is a consequence of material and environment. It must preserve depth and silhouette rather than flatten the campus into visual noise.

## Transparency

Transparency is graduated by purpose: public areas are more open, operational areas selectively visible, and restricted areas intentionally unresolved. Opacity is a valid architectural decision.
